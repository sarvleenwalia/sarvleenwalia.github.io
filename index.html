01 / In my own words

I started Beyond Rote so students could do real research

Beyond Rote started from frustration. I hated rote learning, and I could see how little research reaches school students in India. So I started the thing I wished existed: a research-based community for high schoolers.

I led a team of 6+, and every one of us came from a tier 3 city. We partner with professors from leading research institutes and with schools, so students meet real research early. Beyond Rote grew to over 2,000 members.

Beyond Rote treats students as early researchers rather than recipients of facts. Sessions follow four commitments, and every session is written up as an institutional memory record so the thinking outlasts the people in the room.

03 / One question across eight fields

How do complex systems retain meaningful information despite noise, damage, and uncertainty, and what does this imply about storage, structure, and reconstruction?

Memory is rebuilt, not replayed

Localized damage weakens retrieval, not the existence of memories. Loss tracks how much cortex is damaged more than where (the Principle of Mass Action). The holographic model fits this, but no biological mechanism has been found, and modern neural networks explain distributed processing without it.

Open questionIf memory is physical but non-localized, what exact physical quantity or transformation stores it?

Small geometry changes reshape the whole flow

Shocks form only where flow exceeds the speed of sound. Comparing underexpanded and overexpanded nozzles shows how sensitive these structures are to design. Chevrons and microjets cut low-frequency jet noise, with a trade-off in high frequencies.

Six degrees is an average, not a guarantee

Milgram's letter experiment found surprisingly short chains between strangers. Bridging ties keep the network connected, and socioeconomic stratification did not significantly lengthen the average path.

Design is adaptive thinking under uncertainty

Design differs from planning and from art. Its goals are often unclear at the start and evolve during the work. Professional design is deliberate, and it is reflective rather than instinctive.

04 / Research prototype

Why do dopaminergic neurons die, and can we see it coming?

Parkinson's disease destroys dopaminergic neurons. I study that degeneration and the therapeutic approaches that follow from it, and I explore AI and machine-learning models that predict how stem cells differentiate.

Version 1

  • Dopaminergic neuron degeneration and potential therapies
  • Machine-learning models for stem cell differentiation outcomes
  • Literature reviews and data analysis that support ongoing experiments
  • Work with mentors and online research communities

Machine-Learning Prediction of Stem-Cell Differentiation Fate Using Single-Cell RNA-Seq Data (with application to Parkinson's dopaminergic neurons). Written under Dr Sujata Mohanty. The field lacks standard, scalable pipelines for finding subtle early-stage signals in public, mixed-quality datasets. The paper proposes a pipeline designed to be accessible, reproducible and computationally light. We built a first version, and its model achieved 91% accuracy in our initial evaluation. The project paused because we needed more data, and lab testing is still pending.

  • 1. Curate Parkinson's and healthy-control iPSC-derived neuron scRNA-seq data from GEO and ArrayExpress
  • 2. Normalize and reduce dimensions
  • 3. Cluster cells without labels
  • 4. Test differential gene expression
  • 5. Map early dysregulation in mitochondrial, lysosomal and proteostasis pathways

Goal: early molecular signatures that inform regenerative-therapy strategies and pre-symptomatic interventions.

06 / Shelved

Morpheus, AI infrastructure for predictive science

Scientific experimentation is slow, expensive and unpredictable. Morpheus was my attempt to forecast an experiment's outcome before anyone runs it.

The problem

  • Researchers rely on trial-and-error workflows
  • Outcomes are hard to forecast before lab work starts
  • No unified predictive engine exists for laboratory experiments

The solution

  • A predictive AI engine that models experimental variables and forecasts likely outcomes before physical execution
  • Works across enzyme-based and non-enzyme systems
  • Goal: fewer failed experiments and faster discovery cycles

Business model

  • Subscriptions for labs and research institutions
  • Enterprise licensing for pharma and biotech firms
  • Later, API access for research software platforms

Vision

  • Become the AI operating layer for scientific experimentation
  • Move research from trial and error to predictive engineering

A team in China built it first, so I shelved it. My team slide read: "Focused on AI-driven research infrastructure and making research accessible for all." That is still what I want to work on.
